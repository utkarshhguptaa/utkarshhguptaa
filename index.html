FemCare AI: a PCOS tracker for cycles that don’t follow a calendar

A web app that helps people with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) track cycles and symptoms, understand their risk, and prepare for doctor visits. I led a team of 4 and worked across interface design, product logic, and the machine learning behind it.

The problem

Most cycle trackers are built around a 28-day calendar. With PCOS, cycles are often irregular, so a single predicted date can miss by days or weeks and the app quietly stops being useful.

Four design decisions

1. A forecast that admits when it is unsure

Problem: A single predicted date hides how irregular someone’s cycles are.

Decision: Forecast from a weighted average of recent cycles, and show a variability flag when cycle lengths swing widely.

Why: The flag tells people how much to trust the forecast, instead of letting a confident-looking date mislead them.

2. Explain every risk result

Problem: A bare risk score in a health app is hard to trust and easy to fear.

Decision: Attach a SHAP explanation to each prediction, showing which inputs pushed the result up or down.

Why: People can see the reasons, question them, and raise them with a doctor.

3. Respond to the individual

Problem: Generic diet and exercise tips ignore the person reading them.

Decision: Drive risk, nutrition, and exercise recommendations from each user’s own stats, symptoms, and routine.

Why: Advice only gets used when it fits the person’s actual life.

4. Built for the doctor’s visit

Problem: Appointments are short, and people forget what they logged.

Decision: Export a printable health summary the user can hand to their doctor.

Why: Months of tracking turn into one page that supports a real conversation.

Product principles I held to: free to use, transparent about how results are produced, and no aggressive paywalls.

What I haven’t done yet

I have not run formal usability tests on FemCare AI. Next, I would ask five people to complete two tasks, logging a cycle and finding out why a risk result appeared, note where they hesitate, and revise the screens that cause friction.
